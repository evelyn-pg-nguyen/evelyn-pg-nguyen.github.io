Financial Analysis Dashboard

Overview

A personal Power BI project built to explore how sales, forecast, profitability, COGS and discounts can be brought together into one financial performance view.

Why I Built It

This was one of my first Power BI projects. I built it while learning how data visualization could support the kind of budgeting, forecasting and financial analysis work I was becoming more interested in.

The goal was not to make the dashboard as complicated as possible. I wanted to see whether I could take a simple dataset and make the main business questions easy to answer.

Business Questions

The dashboard was designed around a few questions:

  • Are sales on track against forecast?
  • Which products drive the most revenue, and which actually generate the best margin?
  • Where are discounts and COGS putting pressure on profitability?
  • Which months or products need a closer look?

What I Built

I created a two-page Power BI report with an Overview page for high-level performance and a Detail page for deeper product analysis.

The Overview includes KPI cards for gross sales, forecast sales, forecast accuracy, profit margin and discount impact, together with monthly actual vs forecast performance.

The Detail page goes further into product performance, including sales by month, forecast gaps, COGS, discount impact and profitability.

Users can also filter the report by fiscal year, segment and country.

Key Insights

  • Sales finished at $92.31M against a $94.79M forecast, reaching 97.4% of plan. Performance was below forecast in 8 of 12 months, with the largest miss in December.
  • Paseo generated the most sales, but its margin was weaker than several smaller products, showing why revenue alone does not tell the full story.
  • Montana delivered the strongest profit margin, while VTT had the lowest discount impact.
  • The product view makes it easier to separate high-volume products from products that are actually more efficient at converting sales into profit.

What I Learned

This project changed how I think about dashboards. I originally thought a good dashboard needed more visuals and more complexity. Instead, I learned that simple is often better.

A dashboard is useful when someone can look at it, ask “So what?”, and find the answer without needing me to explain every chart.
