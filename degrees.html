Corporate Finance Institute

Financial Planning & Analysis Professional (FPAP™)

Completed October 2026

Background

I started the FPAP certification after moving from accounting into FP&A. It took me nearly eight months to complete, but I was never really in a rush. My goal was not just another certificate. I wanted something structured to compare with my day-to-day work and help me see where my gaps were.

What I learned

The program covered budgeting, forecasting, variance analysis, financial modeling, data storytelling and business decision-making. It also introduced more technical areas such as driver-based forecasting, Excel data analysis and VBA. Even though I use Python more today, I still think it is useful to understand alternatives like VBA.

What I took from it

I thought I needed to learn more complex models. Instead, the course changed how I think about modeling. A good model is not one that scares people away with complexity. It should be intuitive enough that someone can understand and maintain it without you having to explain every step. Assumptions should be clear, the logic should connect to business drivers, and the output should help someone make a decision.

How I use it today

I use a lot of it directly in my work, from budgets and cash-flow forecasts to variance analysis and management reporting. The biggest change is that I think more carefully about structure before I start building, keeping the assumptions visible, the logic consistent and the final output easy to understand.

For me, the certificate is useful, but what matters more is that what I learned has already found its way into how I work.

Flinders University · Adelaide, Australia

Master of Accounting

December 2021 · GPA 6.81 / 7.00

Background

I completed my Master of Accounting at Flinders University in Australia. At the time, I was building a stronger technical foundation in finance and accounting, including IFRS (International Financial Reporting Standards).

What I learned

The degree gave me a strong accounting foundation, but also helped me understand the nature and purpose of accounting. One of the first things I learned was that accounting is ultimately there to support stakeholders in decision-making.

What I took from it

What stayed with me most was not one particular subject. I could forget some areas that I don't use often, like specific IFRS rules for banking, but the habit of wanting to understand where a number comes from instead of just accepting the final report has stayed with me until now.

How I use it today

That mindset is still very relevant in my FP&A work. Before I forecast, model or explain a variance, I normally want to know whether the accounting underneath it makes sense first. It helps me connect the details at transaction level with the bigger business picture.

Foreign Trade University, Vietnam

Bachelor of Economics, International Business Economics

Graduated 2018 · GPA 3.40 / 4.00

Bachelor of Economics, International Business Economics

Background

This was my first choice of career path. At 18, I thought I wanted to work in international trade, import and export. To be honest, I also chose it because it was one of the most competitive programs to get into at the time, and that activated the competitive side of me.

What I learned

Not everything I learned is directly relevant to what I do today, but this degree laid the first foundation for how I understand a business. I learned the basics across different functions, from sales and marketing to accounting, finance and HR, while focusing more on international trade, including import/export transactions, logistics, transportation, Incoterms, insurance and trade documentation.

My coursework reflected that mix, from Marketing, Accounting and Finance to International Business Transactions, Logistics and International Transportation, Insurance and Risk Management, and International Finance.

Along the way, I also completed the FIATA Diploma in Freight Forwarding in 2017. It gave me a more practical understanding of freight forwarding, transportation, customs, documentation and how international shipments actually move from one place to another.

What I took from it

I learned so many things that I definitely don't remember all of them now. But some very specific details still stay with me. I remember learning about driving and rest-time rules in road transportation, how containers are arranged and secured on a vessel, what happens when a shipment arrives but the required documents are missing, and how responsibility and risk move between the buyer and seller under different shipping terms.

Those details may sound quite far away from FP&A, but they taught me that there is always an operational story behind a number.

How I use it today

I don't need all of that knowledge in my current job, but I still feel comfortable joining a discussion about which Incoterm makes sense, reviewing shipping terms in a purchase contract, or understanding where transportation and cargo insurance risks sit.

I probably won't be the logistics expert in the room anymore, but I know enough to ask the right questions and understand what the operations team is talking about. For me, knowing what you know and what you don't know is very important.
